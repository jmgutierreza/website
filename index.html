01 — About

Background &
Interests

I am a PhD candidate in Economics and Research Associate at Lancaster University, supervised by Prof. Catherine Porter and Dr Saurabh Singhal. My research focuses on development and education economics, with a particular interest in how early-life and school-level factors shape long-run inequality in Latin America. As a Research Associate, I work on health economics with Prof. Heather Brown.

Before joining Lancaster, I completed my M.Sc. and B.A. in Economics at Universidad del Pacífico (Lima, Peru), and worked as a Research Assistant at the World Bank and as a junior researcher at REDES. I have also worked in Peru's public sector, at the Ministries of Education, Environment, and Housing, the National Civil Service Authority (SERVIR), and CONCYTEC. My work has been published in the Journal of Air Transport Management.

Alongside research in applied microeconomics, I have taught a range of economics courses at Universidad del Pacífico and Lancaster University, and have advised undergraduate students on their research projects at Universidad del Pacífico.

Working Paper Gender Education

Girls at the Top of the Class: Classroom Peers and the Gender Gap in STEM

Single-authored · Lancaster University

Girls are under-represented among the top maths students in Peruvian classrooms. Using data on almost one million students, I study whether a higher share of girls at the top of a Grade 2 classroom changes the gender gap in STEM choices at university. A 10 pp higher share of girls at the top in maths narrows the girl–boy gap in the initial STEM choice by 0.3 pp, mostly because boys move away from STEM. Early exposure still matters ten years later.

Working Paper Education Inequality

Who Stays the Course? Early Classroom Rank and Educational Trajectories

With Saurabh Singhal · Lancaster University

Where you rank in Grade 2 shapes whether you stay in school and apply to university a decade later, even holding achievement constant. But rank does not help students get admitted, and the poorest quarter does not respond, even at application.

Presented at: LACEA–LAMES 2026 (Lima) · BREAD/ITAM/CAF 2026 (Mexico City) · RES 2026 (Newcastle) · IWAEE 2026 (Catanzaro) · NWSSDTP PhD Conference 2026 (Liverpool)

Published Air Transport Tourism

More seats, more tourists? Evidence from Peru

Journal of Air Transport Management, 127, 102796 (with Enzo Defilippi)

Peru's tourism boom wasn't just about Machu Picchu — it was about planes. Adding seats on direct flights to the US raised tourist arrivals by 0.36% per 1% increase. And expanding routes to Spain pulled in more tourists from Italy, Switzerland, and Germany too.
